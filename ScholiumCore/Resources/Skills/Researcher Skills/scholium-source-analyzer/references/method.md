# Source-Analysis Method

## 1. Access and analysis unit

Verify bibliographic identity, source type, version, language and translation, what was inspected, and whether access is complete, partial, excerpted, OCR-derived, or unreliable. Never fill missing metadata or locators from memory.

Define the complete session analysis unit before reading:

- normally the whole paper or chapter;
- for a monograph, the whole book only when requested and feasible, otherwise exact chapters or sections;
- for a cluster, one explicit unit per source;
- for partial access, exactly the available fragment.

For full-source analysis, each pass includes notes, appendices, figures, tables, examples, citations, or formal apparatus when they bear on the argument. Distinguish printed from file pagination and visually verify quotations, symbols, and page-specific claims when extraction may be unreliable.

## 2. Evidential layers

Keep these layers visibly separate:

- source-explicit statement, definition, argument, objection, reply, concession, or self-positioning;
- source-supported analyst reconstruction;
- charitable repair or bridging premise supplied by the analyst;
- analyst objection, comparison, or evaluation;
- project-use candidate or unread follow-up lead.

A plausible reconstruction is not a quotation. A charitable repair is not the author's argument. The author's account of a debate is not independently verified field history.

## 3. Orientation pass

Read the complete unit in source order and map:

- the question, pressure, or purpose;
- provisional thesis, governing interpretation, or contribution;
- section structure, movement, and turning points;
- genre, method, intended scope, and authorial debate framing;
- candidate load-bearing concepts, passages, arguments, objections, and replies;
- access gaps, extraction problems, and locator risks.

Do not settle the final interpretation or draft the final report during this pass.

## 4. Analytical pass

Read the complete unit again. Reconstruct at the level appropriate to its philosophical function.

### Concepts and distinctions

For each load-bearing concept, preserve the source's term and locator. Determine whether it is defined, stipulated, inherited, operationalized, exemplified, or left implicit. Distinguish definitions from slogans, examples, criteria, consequences, and nearby theses. Track scope, contrast class, inferential role, ambiguity, and change.

### Arguments and interpretive moves

For each central unit record only applicable fields:

```text
Unit type and provenance:
Claim, conclusion, interpretation, distinction, description, or result:
Grounds: premises, passages, cases, derivation, description, or evidence:
Inferential, interpretive, conceptual, explanatory, formal, or normative link:
Scope, modality, and claimed force:
Hidden assumptions and burden:
Objection or alternative and provenance:
Author's reply, concession, and residual pressure:
Analyst reconstruction, repair, objection, or evaluation:
Locator:
```

Assess the link separately from support for premises or interpretation. A valid inference does not establish its premises; a plausible premise does not repair an invalid bridge.

### Objections

Label each objection as author-raised, source-reported, analyst-generated, or researcher-generated. State its exact target and whether it is internal or external. Give the strongest warranted version, reply if any, reply cost, and residual pressure. Do not invent an objection to make the analysis look comprehensive.

## 5. Genre-sensitive checks

- **Argument-driven:** claims, grounds, inference, objections, replies, burdens, and scope; do not force every support relation into deduction.
- **Historical or exegetical:** primary-text passages, historical context, conceptual change, rival readings, interpretive principle, edition and translation; guard against anachronism.
- **Phenomenological or descriptive:** phenomenon, standpoint, procedure, experiential structure, and claimed generality; separate description from causal, metaphysical, and normative inference.
- **Genealogical, critical, or diagnostic:** target practice or concept, explanatory genealogy, critical standard, and bridge from diagnosis to conclusion.
- **Formal:** syntax, semantics, definitions, assumptions, derivation, model or countermodel, informal target, and adequacy of philosophical interpretation.
- **Normative:** bearer, scope, strength, reasons or values, bridge principles, conflict, priority, feasibility, defeat, and residual demands.
- **Cases and thought experiments:** stipulations, imported assumptions, target verdict, modal status, variants, confounds, and whether the case illustrates, supports, pressures, or refutes.
- **Empirically informed philosophy:** empirical claim, design, result, warranted scope, load-bearing philosophical premise, and bridge; mark specialist checks when needed.
- **Survey or overview:** topic architecture, positions, distinctions, omissions, and organizing narrative; do not present its taxonomy as settled history.

## 6. Review pass

Read the complete source unit a third time while testing the analytical draft against it. Check:

- unsupported load-bearing claims;
- omitted passages, objections, replies, qualifications, examples, or counterevidence;
- concept substitution, terminology drift, equivocation, or collapsed distinctions;
- overstated scope, modality, novelty, consensus, or philosophical force;
- mistaken attribution or dialectical role;
- reconstruction or repair presented as textual fact;
- quotation, pagination, locator, symbol, translation, and edition accuracy;
- evaluation blurred with source report.

Revise from the source outward and record unresolved problems. Proofreading only the draft is not a Review pass.

## 7. Debate context and research role

Reconstruct how the author presents rivals, allies, foils, precursors, and borrowed machinery. Separate this self-positioning from independently checked context.

Assign a possible research role only when supported: primary evidence, interpretation, target, rival, partial ally, conceptual resource, methodological model, case source, objection source, background, or lead. Do not infer support, criticism, influence, or participation from citation, keyword overlap, chronology, graph proximity, or similar wording.

Unread bibliography items remain leads. A project-relevance or debate-importance rating is optional, context-bound, and requires an explicit scope plus checked comparative evidence. It is not a score of truth, quality, prestige, or reliability.

## 8. Completion standard

A complete analysis states its access and scope, reports all three passes, preserves evidential layers, supplies locators for load-bearing source claims, and marks material uncertainty. It does not claim more of the source than the completed unit warrants.
