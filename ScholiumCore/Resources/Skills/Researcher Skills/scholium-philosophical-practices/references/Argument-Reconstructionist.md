# Argument Reconstructionist

Status: Editable default practice  
Primary function: Recover the structure of an argument while distinguishing what is explicit in the source from what is reconstructed by the analyst.

## Purpose

The Argument Reconstructionist makes philosophical reasoning inspectable. It identifies claims, reasons, inferential links, assumptions, and argumentative burdens in a text, dialogue, note, or developing position.

Reconstruction may be charitable, but charity must not be confused with attribution. The strongest reconstruction is still a reconstruction unless the source explicitly makes the relevant move.

## Core question

> What is the argument, which parts are explicit, which parts are inferred, and does the conclusion follow at the asserted strength?

## Foundational dimensions

Primary:

- Logical and Dialectical Reasoning

Supporting:

- Concept-Understanding
- Background-Grasping

This Practice remains constrained by the protected Core Protocol and the shared foundation in the package entry.

## Entry conditions

- A determinate argument, passage, note, or position and its intended conclusion are available.
- The available material is sufficient to distinguish explicit reasons from analyst-supplied repair.
- The task permits reconstruction rather than mere summary.

## Attend to

- The central conclusion or claim.
- Explicit premises and reasons.
- Evidence, examples, analogies, and explanatory material.
- Inferential links and bridge premises.
- Hidden assumptions.
- Scope, modality, and changes in argumentative force.
- Subarguments and dependencies among claims.
- Objections, replies, counterexamples, and alternative reconstructions.
- The difference between validity, soundness, evidential support, explanatory adequacy, and plausibility.

## Procedure

1. Identify the text, note, or position being reconstructed and its intended conclusion.
2. Trace the explicit reasons in their original order where that order matters.
3. Separate premises, conclusions, examples, definitions, background claims, and rhetorical transitions.
4. Mark every premise or inferential link supplied by the analyst rather than the source.
5. Produce the strongest reasonable reconstruction without changing the claim’s scope.
6. Test whether the conclusion follows from the reconstructed premises.
7. Assess whether the premises are supported, and identify where the argument remains vulnerable.
8. Compare an alternative reconstruction when the text permits more than one.
9. State which parts are textual, reconstructed, evaluative, or unresolved.

## Output contract

Return, as appropriate:

- **Target conclusion**
- **Explicit source claims**
- **Reconstructed premises**
- **Inferential links**
- **Hidden or bridge premises**
- **Argument status** — validity, support, strength, or remaining gap
- **Objections or vulnerabilities**
- **Alternative reconstruction**
- **Uncertainty and source locators**

Use a formal premise-conclusion display only when it clarifies rather than distorts the philosophical text.

## Failure conditions

Stop or narrow the result when no argument can be located, the decisive passage is unavailable, rival reconstructions cannot be adjudicated from the evidence, or making the reasoning valid would require inventing a substantially new argument. Return the exact gap rather than attributing the repair to the source.

## Safeguards

- Do not present an analyst-supplied premise as the author’s explicit claim.
- Do not call an argument valid merely because it is coherent or persuasive.
- Do not infer soundness from validity.
- Do not treat an example as a premise unless it performs that role.
- Do not expand a qualified conclusion into an unrestricted one.
- Do not use a charitable repair to hide a genuine textual gap.
- Preserve the distinction between the argument reconstructed and the argument the analyst recommends.

## Boundaries

The Argument Reconstructionist does not:

- determine whether the author’s conclusion is true;
- replace source interpretation when the textual meaning is disputed;
- guarantee that an implicit premise was intended;
- turn a reconstruction into a quotation or source-backed fact.

## Editable points

The researcher may choose a preferred notation, define standards for charity, add argument types, require diagramming, or specify how much formalization is appropriate for different philosophical traditions.
