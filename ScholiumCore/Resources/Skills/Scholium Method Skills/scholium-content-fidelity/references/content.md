# Content-fidelity method

## Establish the check boundary

Bind the audit to the exact Target revision, stated scope, and evidence actually accessible in this run. A source-specific claim requires the relevant source unit or a locator-backed source analysis. Project notes, search snippets, bibliography leads, and prior agent turns may orient the check but cannot establish what a publication says.

Prefer the exact primary source or original philosophical text, then a verified
passage with sufficient context, then a locator-backed source analysis. Use
researcher-authored Notes only to establish their exact wording and authorship,
not what a published source says or what the researcher believes, accepts, or
treats as important. Treat a formulation as a researcher-stated commitment
only when the inspected text or current request explicitly presents it as the
researcher's thesis or commitment, and retain that statement's context.
Authorship, editing, Settle, authorization, selection, or silence alone never
establishes commitment. General background knowledge cannot repair missing
source evidence.

## Separate claim owners

For each material statement distinguish:

- source-explicit wording or position;
- report of another view;
- interpretation or argument reconstruction;
- agent-supplied charitable repair or criticism;
- Topic synthesis;
- researcher-authored wording without inferred commitment;
- researcher-stated commitment with its explicit textual basis;
- researcher-authored candidate, question, or uncertainty;
- agent-authored candidate or recommendation;
- background context whose direct evidential role is unestablished.

Then test terminology, scope, modality, premise and inference structure, objection and reply roles, source role, locator, and visible uncertainty. Do not assimilate a source's distinct terms to local vocabulary without an explicit bridge.

## Check local semantic consistency

Compare each load-bearing term, distinction, polarity, modality, and thesis
status with its definition and repeated uses elsewhere in the exact Target.
Attend especially to paired or negated formulations whose surface fluency can
hide a reversal, and to summaries, headings, introductions, and conclusions
that strengthen or weaken the body. A contradiction that the Target itself
settles can be established from that revision without external source access;
use sources only when deciding which formulation is faithful to a
source-specific claim.

Treat researcher-authored front matter summaries and other authored synopsis
fields as claims about the Target, not as disposable index metadata. Always
compare their controlling thesis and modality with the body's introduction,
argument, qualifications, and conclusion before finalizing the check, even
when source-dependent findings are also available.

Prefer the least demanding coherent reading that the wording and context
permit. Ambiguity, compression, or failure to establish every pairwise
distinction is not by itself a material inconsistency. Report a stronger defect
only when the problematic reading is required by the exact claim, its stated
basis, or its argumentative role.

Preserve the difference between a source-supported reconstruction and the
Target's own explicitly marked proposal, conditional argument, or hypothesis.
The cited sources' failure to establish that proposal is not a fidelity defect
when the Target acknowledges the gap and supplies or requests its own bridge.
If a local conclusion overstates what that bridge has established, correct
only that support or modality claim; do not replace the proposal with the
weaker source-supported position.

Track quantificational force. A claim that several listed standards are not
all one relation does not assert that every pair differs. Require pairwise
support only when the Target makes the pairwise claim.

## Calibrate by claim type

Use only the checks that fit the material:

- a definition: formulation type, scope, contrast class, role, cases, and
  whether an example, criterion, or consequence has been mistaken for a
  definition;
- an interpretation: passage, context, rival reading, edition or translation,
  and any historical or anachronistic assumption;
- a phenomenological description: standpoint, descriptive scope, and any
  unargued explanatory, empirical, normative, or metaphysical transition;
- a formal claim: definitions, assumptions, derivation, interpretation, and
  whether the prose exceeds the formal result;
- a case or thought experiment: stipulations, target, modal role, rival
  description, and whether it illustrates, supports, diagnoses, or refutes;
- an empirically informed claim: reported result, warranted scope, technical
  verification status, bridge premise, and philosophical conclusion.

Content Fidelity audits source and philosophical accuracy. It does not assess
style, publication readiness, workflow status, or whether the strongest
possible objection has been answered unless the Target itself makes a claim
about those matters.

## Report atomically

Identify the exact claim, its asserted basis, the evidence inspected, and one outcome: supported within scope, partly supported, overstated, unsupported, disputed, or source unavailable. Explain the smallest needed correction without editing the Target.

A successful check means that no material defect was found within this declared evidence boundary. It does not establish truth, originality, completeness, researcher acceptance, or philosophical importance.
