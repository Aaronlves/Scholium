# Method fit and philosophical understanding

Use this reference only when philosophical method matters to reconstruction or
evaluation. Distinguish three questions:

1. What question is the source trying to answer?
2. What inputs, procedures, and success conditions does the source actually
   use?
3. Which evaluative pressure is appropriate to that undertaking?

Do not infer a method from discipline labels alone. A source may combine
several methods, leave one implicit, or apply a method unevenly. No method
family below is the universal default, and comparative weakness in one setting
does not by itself disqualify that method elsewhere.

## Conditional method families

Use only the families that illuminate the source:

- **Argumentative or explanatory:** reconstruct premises, inferential links,
  explanatory targets, alternatives, scope, and theoretical costs. Distinguish
  deductive failure from weaker explanatory or abductive support.
- **Conceptual or explicative:** track a concept's role, extension, inferential
  connections, cases, and revision criteria. Distinguish describing a concept
  from proposing how it should change.
- **Historical or exegetical:** use text, language, edition, intellectual
  context, and reception only as available. Test rival readings and
  anachronism without requiring the source to answer a present-day question.
- **Phenomenological or descriptive:** identify the target experience,
  descriptive distinctions, variations, and limits. Do not turn descriptive
  adequacy into a causal, metaphysical, empirical, or normative conclusion
  without a bridge.
- **Genealogical:** identify the target practice or category, the historical or
  social dependencies the genealogy actually traces, its explanatory aim, and
  any claimed debunking or vindicatory consequence. Contingency or provenance
  alone does not establish a normative verdict.
- **Critical or diagnostic:** identify the target, declared critical standard
  or diagnostic criterion, explanatory mechanism, and the defect,
  contradiction, distortion, or practical consequence alleged. Require
  historical dependence only when the source itself uses a genealogical move.
- **Formal:** inspect primitives, definitions, assumptions, derivations,
  interpretation, and scope. A valid derivation does not establish that its
  formalization fits the philosophical target.
- **Normative:** identify the normative target, kind and strength of force,
  reasons or values, bridge principles, conflicts, priority, feasibility,
  defeat conditions, and residual demands. Do not require reflective-equilibrium
  structure unless the source actually uses it.
- **Reflective equilibrium:** distinguish considered judgments, principles,
  background theories, revision moves, and the claimed fit among them. Treat
  the resulting justification as bounded and defeasible rather than
  mechanically guaranteed.
- **Case-based or thought-experimental:** identify stipulations, target claim,
  modal or evidential role, relevant similarity, rival descriptions, and what
  the case could actually establish. A case may illustrate, diagnose, support,
  pressure, or refute; these roles are not interchangeable.
- **Empirically informed or interdisciplinary:** keep the reported finding,
  evidential scope, bridge premise, philosophical conclusion, and disciplinary
  standard distinct. Neither empirical evidence nor philosophical analysis
  silently supplies the missing bridge.
- **Comparative or cross-tradition:** preserve source terms, translation
  choices, historical settings, and asymmetries. Similar vocabulary does not
  establish conceptual identity or a shared problem.

## Evaluation discipline

First judge whether the source uses its own inputs and procedures coherently
and whether they can support its stated aim. Then apply external pressure only
when the comparison is philosophically relevant and its standard is explicit.
Do not criticize one genre merely for lacking the characteristic output of
another.

Explain how the view fits into a network of possibilities: what follows if it
is accepted, which nearby views remain available, where revision would
propagate, and what evidence or argument would discriminate among them. Keep
source-stated consequences separate from agent-derived implications.

Method assessment is an attributed, task-bounded judgment. It does not certify
the source, Analysis, researcher, or project as original, publishable,
doctoral-level, or complete.
