# Synthesize method

The numbered stages below govern selection, integration, and validation. They
are not Topic headings. The saved Topic follows its philosophical problem and
conceptual or dialectical structure rather than the order in which Materials
were inspected.

## 1. Establish the conceptual home and coverage

Read the current Topic before routing new material. Identify its governing question, distinctions, live positions, objections, and unresolved structure. If the requested material has no genuine role here, leave the Topic unchanged and explain why.

State whether the task concerns only the supplied project materials or makes a
field-facing coverage claim. Qualify the resulting prose when known exclusions,
languages or traditions, or likely selection bias materially limit that claim.
Do not turn these limits into a reading inventory, and do not present project
coverage as literature coverage.

## 2. Classify contributions by evidence

For each relevant inspected Material, determine whether it:

- adds a missing concept, argument, case, or distinction;
- corrects an attribution or reconstruction;
- qualifies a claim's scope, force, or evidential status;
- reopens an issue through a live objection, alternative, or unresolved reply.

Do not infer these roles from keyword overlap, backlinks, or selection alone.

Before comparing Materials, check whether their questions, terms, methods, and
levels of analysis are sufficiently commensurable. Preserve a material
asymmetry rather than manufacturing agreement or disagreement.

## 3. Integrate minimally

Place each warranted result under the existing problem-centered structure. Preserve rival possibilities when no basis settles them. Use explicit prose for relations instead of making connectivity carry philosophical meaning. Avoid duplicating a source Analysis inside the Topic.

Trace the resulting network of commitments: which conclusions depend on which
concepts or premises, what changes under each live alternative, and which new
implications are source-stated versus agent-derived. Treat a novel connection
as an attributed candidate until its source and field status are checked.

## 4. Judge a provisional stopping point when in scope

Apply this step only when the researcher asks whether a bounded inquiry has
enough coverage, or when the requested synthesis itself makes a field-facing
coverage claim. Otherwise do not infer a stopping state from the selected
Materials. When it applies, assess separately whether further literature,
cases, interpretations or perspectives, reflective pressure, or theory
construction is still changing the Topic. A dimension may be provisionally
saturated only when recent additions repeat rather than refine the relevant
map and a reasonable counter-search for selection bias has been attempted. An
empty search result, search count, or the selected Materials alone does not
establish saturation.

This is gradual and task-relative. Distinguish:

- **not assessed:** stopping adequacy was outside the task or was not examined;
- **insufficient basis:** there is no actual sequence of additions to compare,
  too little inspected material, or no substantive counter-search;
- **advancing:** new material still changes a material distinction, position,
  objection, case, implication, or limit;
- **practical cutoff:** time, access, language, or scope ends the pass before a
  justified stopping point;
- **provisionally saturated:** the bounded dimension has a stated basis for
  stopping and remains open to defeating material; and
- **blocked or stuck:** the inquiry is not progressing, without evidence that
  the space has been adequately tested.

Never infer complete literature coverage, field consensus, or finality from
any of these states. Without inspected change history and a substantive
counter-search, use insufficient basis rather than a saturation judgment.

## 5. Validate

Reread the Topic and check that source-facing statements remain traceable,
agent evaluation remains attributed, coverage is not overstated, and the
update has not silently converted a candidate into the researcher's settled
view.
