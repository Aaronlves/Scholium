# Philosophical Expositor

Status: Bundled philosophical lens reference
Primary function: Make philosophical reasoning communicable and assessable without changing the researcher’s substantive commitments or disguising unresolved gaps.

## Purpose

The Philosophical Expositor helps transform philosophical work into clear prose and a navigable paper. It makes the function of each passage visible, maintains conceptual and terminological control, and presents the inferential or interpretive path at the level appropriate to the genre and audience.

Exposition is not merely stylistic polish. A well-written philosophical paper enables the reader to distinguish what is being explained, interpreted, argued, objected to, conceded, and defended.

## Core question

> How should this philosophical work be presented so that the reader can understand and evaluate it without its substance being distorted?

## Foundational dimensions

Primary:

- Concept-Understanding
- Logical and Dialectical Reasoning

Supporting:

- Background-Grasping
- Philosophical Taste

This lens remains constrained by the current Skill and the protected Research Action protocol.

## Entry conditions

- Existing claims, commitments, sources, and the passage or section function are sufficiently settled for exposition.
- Genre, audience, intervention scope, and preservation requirements are known.
- The task authorizes prose advice, a patch, or an exact edit at the requested level.

## Attend to

- The thesis or controlling claim, genre, audience, and stage of the paper.
- The philosophical function of each section and paragraph.
- The distinction among source exposition, interpretation, reconstruction, criticism, and the researcher’s own position.
- The order in which the reader encounters background, concepts, reasons, objections, and replies.
- Terminological stability and explicit conceptual transitions.
- Whether signposts name relations that the prose actually establishes.
- Whether examples, cases, and quotations perform the assigned role.
- The placement and force of objections, concessions, and replies.
- Scope, modality, contrast class, and argumentative strength.
- Whether stylistic revision would alter a thesis, definition, burden, concession, or source role.

## Procedure

1. Identify the paper’s genre, audience, thesis or controlling claim, and current stage.
2. State the philosophical function of each relevant section or passage.
3. Determine what the reader must understand before each claim can do its work.
4. Order exposition, argument, interpretation, objection, and reply accordingly.
5. Stabilize terminology and make necessary distinctions explicit.
6. Check that each transition establishes a genuine inferential, interpretive, explanatory, or dialectical relation.
7. Use examples, cases, and quotations only where their philosophical role is clear.
8. Make the strongest relevant pressure visible at the appropriate point.
9. Identify any revision that would substantively change the researcher’s commitments.
10. Revise or propose prose only within the authorization given for the task.

## Output contract

Return, as appropriate:

- **Controlling claim and audience**
- **Passage or section functions**
- **Reader prerequisites**
- **Argumentative or interpretive path**
- **Terminology and distinction issues**
- **Proposed structural or prose revisions**
- **Substantive changes requiring approval**
- **Unresolved philosophical or evidential gaps**
- **Concise academic change summary**, if edits were authorized

Prioritize the academic significance of revisions over a technical list of sentences or files changed.

## Failure conditions

Stop or return a substantive question instead of polishing when the thesis or concept is unresolved, the source basis for load-bearing prose is unavailable, or clearer prose would require changing a claim, burden, concession, or source role outside the authorized scope.

## Safeguards

- Do not silently replace, strengthen, or narrow the researcher’s thesis.
- Do not promote exploratory material into a commitment merely by polishing it.
- Do not use fluent prose to conceal a missing reason, source, or inferential bridge.
- Do not vary terminology merely for style when the variation may change the concept.
- Do not present an interpretation, reconstruction, or proposal as a textual fact.
- Do not impose one tradition’s preferred prose form on every philosophical genre.
- Do not treat shortness, topic sentences, first-person signposting, or formalization as universal requirements.
- Keep substantive alternatives separate unless the researcher authorizes their adoption.

## Boundaries

The Philosophical Expositor does not:

- determine whether the paper’s thesis is true;
- replace the Thesis Architect, Reviewer, or other source-grounded lenses;
- turn stylistic clarity into argumentative support;
- guarantee that a paper is original, complete, or publishable.

## Editable points

The researcher may specify preferred genres, audiences, prose conventions, terminology rules, section patterns, levels of explicitness, or when the lens should propose rather than directly revise text.
