# Genre-sensitive drafting and revision

Use this reference only when the Work requires substantial drafting,
genre-sensitive treatment, long-form control, or substantive revision.

## Establish the writing object

Identify the genre, audience, stage, whole-Work thesis or controlling
interpretation, local passage claim, and the passage's philosophical function.
State what the reader must already understand and what this unit must establish,
interpret, describe, explain, pressure, concede, or resolve.

For substantial work, build two distinct aids when useful:

1. a logical, interpretive, or dialectical outline of what must be established;
2. a prose or section outline of when the reader should encounter it.

Do not require either outline for a bounded sentence or paragraph revision.

## Route by philosophical function

The routes below are illustrative rather than exhaustive. Preserve the Work's
own declared method and success conditions when it uses another philosophical
form or a justified hybrid.

- **Argumentative:** make the target claim, grounds, support relation, scope,
  strongest pressure, reply, and reply cost visible without forcing deductive
  form on abductive, analogical, transcendental, or interpretive
  reasoning.
- **Historical or exegetical:** separate textual claim, interpretive proposal,
  rival reading, textual evidence, historical context, and present significance.
- **Phenomenological or descriptive:** separate description, case, conceptual
  articulation, explanation, and any normative or metaphysical inference.
- **Genealogical, critical, or diagnostic:** make the target practice or
  category, operative historical or explanatory relation, declared critical
  standard or diagnostic criterion, and any debunking, vindicatory, normative,
  or practical consequence explicit without treating provenance as a verdict.
- **Formal:** define notation and assumptions, explain the derivation, map it
  back to the informal claim, and state the formalization's limits.
- **Normative:** identify the normative target, kind and strength of force,
  reasons or values, bridge premises, conflicts, defeat conditions, and the
  response or judgment favored.
- **Case- or thought-experiment-driven:** state stipulations, imported
  assumptions, intended verdict, modal role, target principle, nearby variants,
  and what the case cannot establish.
- **Empirically informed:** keep the scientific claim and scope, technical
  verification need, bridge premise, and philosophical conclusion distinct.
- **Comparative or cross-tradition:** preserve source terminology, translation
  choices, historical asymmetries, and different problem settings; establish
  functional comparability before writing agreement, disagreement, influence,
  or conceptual identity.
- **Literature or debate exposition:** organize by questions, positions,
  attractions, pressures, and warranted source roles rather than an author
  catalogue, unless chronology itself explains the debate.

## Control arguments and definitions

For an argument repair, identify whether the defect is an unsupported premise,
weak inference, equivocation, missing bridge, overstrong conclusion, scope or
modal shift, burden shift, or mismatch between evidence and claim. Keep every
new premise or stronger alternative visibly agent-proposed.

For a definition or distinction, identify whether it is a necessary or
sufficient condition, biconditional analysis, stipulation, explication,
ordinary-language characterization, source-derived definition, or working
formulation. Check scope, contrast class, role, central and edge cases,
counterexamples, circularity, overbreadth, underbreadth, and nearby terms only
as appropriate to that type.

When a proposed definition is load-bearing, state the current and proposed
formulations, what changes in scope, force, role, or commitment, and which
arguments, objections, cases, source interpretations, or sections would be
affected. Do not silently propagate the change beyond the authorized Work
scope; keep dependent changes as explicit candidates when they are not
authorized.

## Revise in philosophical priority order

1. thesis or controlling-claim mismatch;
2. missing reason, textual ground, or inferential bridge;
3. scope, modality, equivocation, or concept drift;
4. unsupported or overextended source claim;
5. unanswered objection or rival reading;
6. unclear passage or section function;
7. transition or structure that names a relation without establishing it;
8. sentence-level awkwardness.

After revising, confirm that every major section still performs a visible
philosophical function, source roles remain warranted, and surface fluency has
not hidden a substantive alternative or unresolved burden.
