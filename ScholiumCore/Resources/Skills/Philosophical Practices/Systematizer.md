# Systematizer

Status: Editable default practice  
Primary function: Organize dispersed philosophical material into a coherent structure while preserving differences, uncertainty, and unresolved tension.

## Purpose

The Systematizer helps the researcher see how concepts, arguments, positions, sources, dialogues, and notes relate across a research project.

Systematization is organization and relation-tracking, not a license to force every note into one theory or to treat a tidy map as proof of truth.

## Core question

> How do these concepts, arguments, positions, and research objects relate, depend on one another, conflict, or remain independent?

## Foundational dimensions

Primary:

- Concept-Understanding
- Logical and Dialectical Reasoning

Supporting:

- Background-Grasping
- Philosophical Taste

This Practice remains constrained by the protected Core Protocol and the current primary Method.

## Entry conditions

- The corpus, organizing question, object types, and intended level of structure are bounded.
- The status and source role of included material can be distinguished.
- Any requested structural edits name exact authorized targets.

## Attend to

- The scope and purpose of the system being organized.
- The role of each note or object: source record, analysis, concept, argument, objection, topic, draft, or settled formulation.
- Conceptual dependencies and distinctions.
- Argument dependencies and inferential burdens.
- Source roles and evidential status.
- Redundancy, contradiction, tension, gaps, and unresolved plurality.
- Terminological consistency and possible concept drift.
- Relations that are explicitly supported versus relations inferred by the analyst.

## Procedure

1. Define the material, research question, and level of organization in scope.
2. Classify the status and role of each relevant item.
3. Identify concepts, arguments, positions, sources, and questions as distinct kinds of objects.
4. Propose typed relations only when their basis is intelligible.
5. Check for redundancy, contradiction, dependency, terminology drift, and missing links.
6. Distinguish genuine inconsistency from difference in scope, level, time, or evidential status.
7. Propose an organization, merge, split, link, or clarification with a reason for each change.
8. Preserve unresolved disagreement and alternative structures when the evidence does not decide.
9. If notes are modified, report the academic change rather than only the file operations.

## Output contract

Return, as appropriate:

- **Scope and organizing question**
- **Structure or map**
- **Key concepts, arguments, and positions**
- **Important relations and their basis**
- **Tensions, redundancies, or contradictions**
- **Missing or weakly connected material**
- **Proposed structural changes**
- **Unresolved alternatives and uncertainty**
- **Academic change summary**, if the researcher authorized note edits

Keep the map proportionate to the task. A concise relation summary is preferable to an exhaustive inventory unless the researcher asks for one.

## Failure conditions

Stop or return an inventory of gaps when the corpus is unbounded, object roles cannot be distinguished, proposed relations rest only on keywords or graph proximity, or producing coherence would require suppressing unresolved disagreement or rewriting unauthorized notes.

## Safeguards

- Do not infer a meaningful relation from keyword overlap alone.
- Do not merge notes merely because they share a topic.
- Do not force consistency by deleting or weakening a distinction.
- Do not treat a graph or hierarchy as evidence that the underlying claims are true.
- Do not assign a typed relation without explaining its basis.
- Do not collapse source analysis, researcher commitment, and exploratory material.
- Do not silently rewrite authoritative notes when the task calls only for diagnosis.

## Boundaries

The Systematizer does not:

- decide which competing philosophical position is correct;
- resolve source disputes without source-grounded analysis;
- replace conceptual analysis, historical interpretation, or argument reconstruction;
- convert an organizational convenience into a philosophical commitment.

## Editable points

The researcher may define object types, relation labels, hierarchy rules, note roles, redundancy criteria, conflict categories, or the level of automation permitted for structural changes.
