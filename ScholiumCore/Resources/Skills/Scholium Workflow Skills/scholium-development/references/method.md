# Development Method

## Purpose

Advance one Analysis or Topic by resolving the philosophical burden actually present. Exploration, concept work, argument work, synthesis, and expression are techniques inside one function, not separate interface activities.

## Fix status and basis

Classify the target item as source-explicit, source-reported, source-supported reconstruction, charitable repair, researcher commitment, researcher hypothesis, agent proposal, or unresolved material. Identify which primary sources, verified passages, Analyses, Topics, Dialogue records, or Comments bear on it and what each may establish.

A note records content and commitments; it is not independent evidence for what a source says. If a load-bearing attribution depends on unavailable source text, narrow the result or stop. Do not recreate Source Analysis as a hidden function.

## Choose the intellectual operation

Use philosophical judgment rather than keyword routing:

- orient when the question, background problem, stakes, or candidate direction is unclear;
- clarify when a concept, definition, distinction, translation, scope, or contrast class is unstable;
- construct or repair when a claim, premise, inference, objection, reply, concession, or burden needs development;
- synthesize when the task concerns warranted relations among several already inspected Materials;
- express when the philosophical content is sufficiently stable and the task is to incorporate it into the Target.

Several may be needed in sequence. Recompute assumptions, evidence, and permission at each transition. A result from one operation is a labeled handoff, not automatically accepted knowledge.

## Develop concepts

Identify the formulation type: necessary or sufficient condition, biconditional, stipulation, explication, ordinary-language characterization, source-derived definition, working formulation, inferential role, historical use, or practical function. Test scope, contrast class, modality, neighboring concepts, ambiguity, central and limiting cases, overbreadth, underbreadth, circularity, explanatory gain, and downstream cost.

Do not mistake a slogan, example, diagnostic mark, consequence, or necessary condition for a complete definition. Preserve source terminology before translating it into the researcher's vocabulary.

## Develop arguments and dialectic

Reconstruct the target conclusion, premises or grounds, support type, bridge principles, assumptions, scope, modality, claimed force, burden, and dependencies. Diagnose whether pressure concerns premise support, inference, equivocation, scope drift, an explanatory-level shift, source use, or conflict with another commitment.

Give objections determinate targets and provenance. A reply may deny, distinguish, narrow, reinterpret, concede, or accept a cost. State what each response preserves, gives up, and leaves under pressure. Restating the thesis is not a reply.

## Compare candidates

Compare source fidelity, conceptual cost, inferential strength, explanatory gain, dialectical availability, compatibility with researcher commitments, revision cost, and residual pressure. Do not choose a substantive position on the researcher's behalf. Recommendations must expose reasons and tradeoffs and remain agent evaluations.

## Stop conditions

Stop or return candidates when evidence is missing, source and researcher positions cannot be separated, a dependent Target lies outside the write set, the task has become Work revision or Critique, or partial adoption would leave the research record misleading.
