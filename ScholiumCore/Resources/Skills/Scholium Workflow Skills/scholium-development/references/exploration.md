# Conditional Exploration

Use when the Target needs orientation rather than an immediate solution.

Preserve the researcher's initial question, then bound the object, tradition, period, source set, level of analysis, and intended use. Reconstruct the pressure that makes the problem live and distinguish positions by motivation, commitment, gain, and cost.

Test whether an apparent tension is conceptual, verbal, historical, methodological, at different explanatory levels, or genuinely incompatible. Explain stakes with reasons; keep novelty, importance, and field-history claims unverified unless the relevant literature was inspected.

For each candidate direction state the problem addressed, evidence basis, potential significance, strongest risk, required check, and what would count against it. End with the smallest next step that could materially reduce uncertainty.
