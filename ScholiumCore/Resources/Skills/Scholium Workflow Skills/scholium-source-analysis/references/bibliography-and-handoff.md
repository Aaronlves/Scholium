# Bibliography Screening and Handoff Candidates

Use this reference only after the analyzed unit has completed the required three passes.

## Bibliography screening

Retain a cited source only when the analyzed source gives a determinate reason to follow it, such as:

- defining or clarifying a load-bearing concept;
- supporting, attacking, or qualifying a premise;
- supplying an important objection, reply, case, or historical turning point;
- representing a major position or a relevant recent or foundational intervention;
- clarifying the source's dialectical target or debate framing.

Do not recommend an item merely because it appears in a bibliography. Separate checked sources from unread leads:

```text
Verified source worth using:
Verified bibliographic identity:
Role established by checked material:
Relevant locator:

Follow-up lead requiring verification:
Probable identity:
Why it may matter:
What must be checked:
```

Use a researcher-installed citation skill when exact style, edition, quotation, DOI, ISBN, or publication metadata requires specialist verification.

## Handoff candidates

A handoff candidate is a bounded proposal, never a file update or accepted source relation. For each candidate state:

```text
Candidate target:
Proposed source role:
Source claim, argument, concept, case, or passage:
Stable locator:
Verification state:
Concrete use:
What this source is not evidence for:
Limitation or misuse risk:
Required next check:
Researcher decision or authorization needed:
```

Prefer one well-supported candidate to a long list of thematic overlaps. Do not infer support, criticism, influence, or debate participation from keyword similarity, citation, chronology, or graph proximity alone.
