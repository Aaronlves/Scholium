# Content Fidelity Method

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest evidence appropriate to each claim: inspected primary source or authoritative edition; verified passage with stable locator and context; source-analysis report whose claims are locator-backed or explicitly reconstructed; current researcher-authored notes for the researcher's own commitments; cautious general background only where it is not used for source-specific attribution.

Project notes, memory, search snippets, bibliography leads, and prior agent responses are context or leads, not evidence of what a published source says. Missing evidence yields unverified or a narrower claim, never invention.

## Classify the target

Classify each material item as a definition or distinction; source-explicit claim; source report of another view; interpretation or historical claim; description; formal or normative bridge; case role; source-supported reconstruction; charitable repair; evidential-role claim; comparison; criticism; synthesis; researcher commitment; or agent proposal.

For each claim assign verified, partly supported, overstated, unsupported, or source missing. Test exact wording, strength, scope, modality, terminology, source role, inferential link, objection-reply role, locator, and uncertainty.

Compare every claim with the declared Research Unit. Flag a claim that exceeds it or a Research Unit that overstates actual coverage; do not silently rewrite the property.

## Genre-sensitive checks

Historical work requires passage context, edition, translation, chronology, conceptual change, rival reading, and anachronism checks. Genealogical work must distinguish historical sequence, causal or functional explanation, critical standard, and the bridge from genealogy to evaluation. Comparative work must preserve non-equivalence, selection basis, translations, and asymmetrical context. Phenomenological work must distinguish description from explanatory or normative inference. Formal work requires definitions, assumptions, derivation, models, interpretation, and the prose bridge. Normative work requires target, bearer, force, reasons, bridge, conflicts, defeat, feasibility, and residual demands. Case-driven work must test stipulations, imported assumptions, target verdict, variants, modal status, and whether a case illustrates, supports, pressures, or refutes. Conceptual engineering must distinguish diagnosis, target function, revision criterion, implementation burden, and cost. Empirically informed work must separate result, technical reliability, warranted scope, bridge, and philosophical consequence.

Audit novelty, research-gap, field-coverage, and missing-citation claims explicitly. Absence from selected Materials, a local graph, one bibliography, or a bounded search cannot establish field absence. Require the documented repositories or corpora, queries, languages, cutoff dates, and inclusion basis appropriate to the claim; otherwise narrow it to a candidate gap or absence from current materials.

## Findings

Use BLOCKER for central false attribution, serious conceptual misuse, invalid reconstruction, or unsupported evidential role; MAJOR for significant overstatement or missing support; MINOR for a needed qualification or locator repair; SUGGESTION for an optional improvement.

A `passed` outcome means no material defect was found within the stated scope and evidence. It does not guarantee truth, originality, importance, completeness, researcher Settlement, or Critique approval.
