# Scholium Research Synthesis

Apply `scholium-core-protocol`. Synthesis must preserve disagreement and evidential layers rather than force all material into one narrative.

## Establish the synthesis packet

```text
Mode: synthesize
Research question:
Corpus boundary:
Included evidential layers:
Intended use:
Relation types needed:
Read set:
Write set:
Permission:
Output:
Stop condition:
Durability:
```

Do not broaden the corpus because a related keyword, backlink, or graph path appears.

## Load the method

Read [references/method.md](references/method.md) completely.

## Apply selected Philosophical Practices

If an explicit Practice is selected, load only that researcher-owned reference plus `COMPOSITION-RULES.md`, and record its stable ID and package revision; otherwise load no Practice. Compatible Practices are `systematizer`, `research-explorer`, `conceptual-analyst`, `historical-interpreter`, and `argument-reconstructionist`.

## Execute

1. Fix the research question, corpus, and intended use.
2. Classify every included item by evidential and authorial layer.
3. Map background, positions, concepts, arguments, objections, replies, cases, and gaps.
4. Give every important relation an explicit type and evidence basis.
5. Preserve unresolved alternatives and incompatible reconstructions.
6. Identify central dependencies, pressures, omissions, and risks of false synthesis.
7. If the researcher requests large-corpus prioritization, calibrate Debate Importance only across one explicit common Debate Scope; otherwise do not add ratings.
8. End with a small set of decisions or verification tasks.

## Return

```text
Research question and corpus:
Materials and evidential layers:
Background and motivations:
Positions and commitments:
Conceptual map:
Argument and dependency map:
Objections, replies, concessions, and costs:
Source roles and relation evidence:
Debate-importance calibration, if requested:
Tensions and unresolved alternatives:
Evidence gaps and risks of false synthesis:
Promising directions:
Bounded next decisions:
```

Do not treat a synthesis map as a settled thesis, a primary source, or authorization to rewrite notes.
