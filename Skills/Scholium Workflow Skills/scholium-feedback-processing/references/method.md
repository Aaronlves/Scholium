# Feedback-Processing Method

## 1. Preserve the feedback record

Keep the original item available. If concision is needed, provide a faithful paraphrase and mark it as such. Separate:

- explicit request;
- stated reason;
- underlying philosophical or communicative concern;
- example or evidence offered;
- implied demand;
- uncertainty about the reader's meaning.

Do not make the reader's comment more decisive, charitable, sophisticated, or coherent than the evidence permits.

## 2. Locate the pressure

Identify the exact target and version. Distinguish:

- local wording or exposition;
- structure or passage function;
- thesis, scope, or contribution;
- concept, definition, or distinction;
- premise, inference, objection, or reply;
- interpretation, source role, quotation, or citation;
- method, audience, or project-management concern.

State whether the concern pressures the central thesis, a local burden, or only reader accessibility.

## 3. Classify the issue

Use the narrowest useful category:

- philosophical significance;
- background or historical context;
- conceptual precision;
- argument or inference;
- source fidelity or evidence;
- dialectical adequacy;
- structure or exposition;
- scope or method;
- citation or bibliographic detail;
- process or project management.

One comment may have several consequences, but do not fragment it into redundant items.

## 4. Test the feedback

Ask:

- Does the target text support the reader's diagnosis?
- Does the feedback identify a defect, a plausible concern, an optional improvement, or a preference?
- Does it rest on a misunderstanding that the prose should nevertheless prevent?
- Does it conflict with another comment or researcher commitment?
- Would accepting it change thesis, scope, definition, source role, inference, or contribution?
- Does the reader's proposed remedy address the actual concern?
- What residual pressure remains even if the proposed change is made?

Authority, seniority, and confidence do not replace reasons. Equally, a reader's misunderstanding may reveal a genuine expository failure even when the philosophical claim is sound.

## 5. Use canonical dispositions

Recommend exactly one token per item:

- `accepted`;
- `partly_accepted`;
- `rejected`;
- `deferred`;
- `clarification_needed`;
- `conflicting`;
- `already_addressed`;
- `not_applicable`.

The agent recommends; the researcher decides. Record disagreement and its reason. Use `clarification_needed` when reliable interpretation is impossible and `conflicting` when comments cannot jointly be satisfied without a substantive choice.

## 6. Create bounded follow-up work

For accepted or partly accepted feedback, specify:

```text
Exact target:
Intended change:
Philosophical burden:
What must be preserved:
Sources or notes to check:
Workflow to use:
Audit or researcher-installed specialist checks to rerun:
Stop condition:
```

Do not turn vague feedback into a broad rewrite. Start a new development, writing, source-analysis, researcher-installed specialist, or review packet for the revision itself; do not inherit permission from feedback processing.

Keep private reasoning distinct from author-facing or referee-facing response language. A response should explain the decision accurately without pretending that every suggestion was accepted.
