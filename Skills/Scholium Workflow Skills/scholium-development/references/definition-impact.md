# Definition and Conceptual Impact

Use when a proposed formulation may alter several arguments, source readings, objections, or notes.

Compare the current and candidate formulations by provenance, extension, intension, contrast class, modality, role, and epistemic status. Trace consequences for premises, conclusions, bridge principles, source interpretations, objections, replies, cases, dependent sections, and the researcher's stated commitments.

Report what changes, what survives, revision cost, terminology or thesis drift, and the decision required. Do not propagate a candidate merely because it is cleaner. Dependent notes remain read-only Materials unless separately targeted in later runs.
