## 1. Preservation and function

State the passage's local function in one sentence: orient, motivate, define, distinguish, reconstruct, interpret, describe, claim, argue, object, reply, concede, limit, compare, synthesize, or transition.

Identify:

- the researcher's intended thesis or controlling claim;
- larger-project thesis when relevant;
- source positions and evidential roles;
- exploratory, proposed, accepted, rejected, or unresolved status;
- terminology that must remain stable;
- requested intervention level.

If the researcher has not yet adopted a thesis, label any drafted controlling claim as a candidate. Do not make authorship appear settled through first-person prose alone.

Flag separately any proposed change to thesis, conclusion, definition, scope, modality, burden, concession, objection-reply structure, source attribution, source role, or status. Do not hide a substantive change inside smoother prose.

## 2. Philosophical structure

For substantial prose:

1. State what the reader must understand first.
2. State what the passage must establish, interpret, describe, or explain.
3. Identify premises, passages, examples, values, or explanatory considerations.
4. Make the inferential, interpretive, explanatory, formal, or normative link visible.
5. State scope, modality, contrast class, and definitions where they matter.
6. Present the strongest relevant pressure or rival reading.
7. Reply, concede, distinguish, narrow, or leave the pressure explicitly unresolved.
8. Explain how the passage advances the larger inquiry.

Use a logical or dialectical outline to control what must be established and a prose outline to control presentation. Do not force an outline for a small sentence-level request.

## 3. Genre calibration

### Argument-driven prose

Make claims, grounds, support relations, scope, objections, and replies explicit enough for the audience.

### Historical or exegetical prose

Separate textual claim, interpretive claim, rival reading, passage evidence, historical context, and present significance. Preserve edition, translation, and anachronism concerns.

### Phenomenological or descriptive prose

Separate description, example, conceptual articulation, explanation, and any normative or metaphysical conclusion.

### Genealogical, critical, or diagnostic prose

State the target, genealogy or diagnosis, critical standard, and the force and limits of the conclusion.

### Formal philosophy

Define notation and assumptions, explain the derivation, and state the philosophical interpretation and limits.

### Normative philosophy

Identify normative target, claim type, force, reasons or values, bridge premises, scope, conflicts, defeat, feasibility, and residual demands.

### Cases and thought experiments

State stipulations, imported assumptions, intended verdict, modal role, target principle, nearby variants, and what the case can and cannot establish.

### Empirically informed philosophy

Separate scientific claim, warranted scope, bridge premise, and philosophical conclusion. Do not turn the passage into an imitation scientific report.

### Literature and debate exposition

Organize by questions, positions, motivations, pressures, and warranted source roles. Avoid an author catalogue unless chronology is itself explanatory.

### Long-form work

Distinguish global thesis from local burdens. Make each section's function and dependency visible. Draft introductions late when the core argument remains unstable.

## 4. Drafting and revision

Work in this order:

1. diagnose the substantive job and problem;
2. verify source support early for premises or interpretations the revision relies on;
3. repair the logical, interpretive, conceptual, or explanatory structure;
4. repair section and paragraph order;
5. tighten scope, terminology, examples, and measured evaluative language;
6. clean citation form, punctuation, rhythm, and surface style last;
7. recheck that the revision did not alter thesis or status silently.

For definitions, distinguish necessary, sufficient, biconditional, stipulative, explicative, source-derived, and working formulations. For objections, name the exact target, force, reply strategy, cost, and residual pressure.

Do not treat a citation as a substitute for reasoning. Do not invent a quotation, locator, bibliographic fact, premise, or transition to make the prose flow.

## 5. Failure conditions

Stop, narrow, or separate alternatives when:

- the required source or argument is unavailable;
- the requested polish would conceal a substantive defect;
- the current thesis cannot be identified;
- two plausible revisions express different philosophical commitments;
- the task requires independent review rather than writing or revision;
- the task is limited to meaning-preserving Prose Control but no selected researcher-owned method is available;
- file permission or target revision is uncertain.
