# Definition and Conceptual-Formulation Impact

Use when changing one definition or conceptual formulation may alter several arguments, source readings, objections, or notes.

## Establish the impact packet

```text
Target concept:
Current formulation and authorial status:
Candidate formulation and provenance:
Reason for considering the change:
Research Unit and sources bearing on it:
Permission: compare-only | candidate-only | direct-edit-authorized
```

## Trace consequences

Compare old and new formulations across:

- extension, intension, contrast class, modality, and theoretical role;
- premises, conclusions, inferential bridges, and argumentative burdens;
- source interpretations and terminology bridges;
- objections, replies, concessions, and counterexamples;
- dependent notes, sections, links, and status claims;
- exclusions or cases newly admitted;
- compatibility with the researcher's stated thesis.

## Return

```text
Old formulation:
Candidate formulation:
Conceptual difference:
Arguments affected:
Sources and interpretations affected:
Objections and replies affected:
Notes or sections affected:
Revision cost:
Risk of terminology or thesis drift:
What remains unchanged:
Researcher decision needed:
```

Do not propagate the candidate merely because it appears cleaner. A load-bearing definition remains a proposal until the task authorizes adoption and the researcher can see its philosophical cost.
