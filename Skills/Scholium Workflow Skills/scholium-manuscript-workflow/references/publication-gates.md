# Manuscript Completion and Publication Gates

Use these gates diagnostically. They identify remaining burdens; they do not guarantee truth, originality, acceptance, or publication.

For every assessed gate keep two axes separate:

- evidence state: `present`, `missing`, `requires-researcher-decision`, or `not-applicable`;
- gate judgment: `pass`, `partial`, `blocked`, or `not-assessed`.

Evidence presence does not entail philosophical adequacy. Bind both axes to the exact manuscript fingerprint.

## Gate 1 — Question and contribution

- The research question and philosophical stakes are explicit.
- The thesis or controlling claim has a defensible scope and status.
- Any novelty or literature-positioning claim has an identified verification basis.
- The manuscript carries its own argumentative burden and does not silently depend on private project architecture or unexplained local terminology.

## Gate 2 — Source basis

- Load-bearing attributions, quotations, interpretations, and source roles are checked at stable locators where available.
- Unavailable or disputed evidence is visible.
- Citation style is checked by a researcher-selected specialist when required.
- Empirical or interdisciplinary material separates the reported result, warranted scope, bridge premise, and philosophical conclusion; load-bearing technical validity is verified or marked for specialist checking.

## Gate 3 — Conceptual control

- Central concepts, distinctions, translations, and local stipulations are stable.
- Material ambiguities and changes of sense are disclosed.

## Gate 4 — Argument and dialectic

- Principal premises, bridges, conclusions, objections, replies, concessions, and residual burdens are inspectable.
- The conclusion does not exceed the reasons or declared Research Unit.

## Gate 5 — Architecture and exposition

- Each section has a necessary philosophical function.
- The reader can distinguish exposition, interpretation, reconstruction, criticism, and the author's own position.
- Prose revision has not concealed a substantive gap or changed the thesis silently.

## Gate 6 — Exact-version integrity

- The final substantive fingerprint has one completed content audit for the required scope.
- Blockers and major findings are resolved, accepted as explicit limitations, or left for a researcher decision.

## Gate 7 — Independent criticism and feedback

- An independent philosophical review has been considered when appropriate to the stage.
- Received feedback has explicit dispositions; accepted items have bounded revisions and regression checks.

## Gate 8 — Researcher decision

- Evidence state and gate judgment are reported separately.
- Remaining uncertainty, venue requirements, and external-action consequences are visible.
- Only the researcher decides whether to circulate, submit, upload, or publish.

## Gate 9 — Venue and submission artifact

- Current authoritative venue instructions have been checked for audience, length, anonymization, abstract, keywords, reference style, file format, permissions, acknowledgements, disclosures, and companion materials where applicable.
- Venue rules were checked with public-safe queries that did not expose private manuscript content.
- The artifact proposed for submission is the exact audited and reviewed manuscript version, or every later change has been rechecked at the required scope.
- Missing citation-style or source-type expertise is routed to a researcher-selected specialist rather than guessed.
- This gate may report readiness for a researcher decision; it never authorizes an upload or submission.

## Gate report

```text
Gate:
Manuscript fingerprint:
Evidence state: present | missing | requires-researcher-decision | not-applicable
Gate judgment: pass | partial | blocked | not-assessed
Evidence:
Unresolved issue:
Required action:
Researcher decision:
```

The final agent statement may be **ready for researcher submission decision**. It must not be **approved for submission**, **publication ready**, or an equivalent claim of authority.
