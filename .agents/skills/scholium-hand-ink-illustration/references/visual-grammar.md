# Scholium hand-ink visual grammar

This reference defines the product-illustration system. It does not redefine
the application icon, interface palette, or production acceptance status.

## 1. Identity source: the pointing-hand icon

Resolve the current canonical application icon from live specification and
packaging construction. Extract its reusable drawing grammar, not its exact
composition:

- deep blue-black or carbon-like ink
- a confident outer contour with slight pressure and width variation
- rounded, human terminals and mildly imperfect joins
- simplified but readable fingers, wrist, and historical cuff
- warm paper showing through the unfilled hand
- sparse manuscript marks rather than detailed scenery
- subtle paper tooth that supports the ink without becoming a vintage filter

The recurring product-art motif is **a hand doing intellectual work**. Useful
gestures include offering, releasing, tracing, holding, parting, weighing,
turning, and reaching. Do not repeat the icon's exact pose, surrounding marks,
or composition. Do not use a hand when another subject makes the action
clearer.

## 2. Flat relational construction

Borrow method, not house style:

- make the whole background one deliberate opaque color field
- reduce the scene to one action and a few simple shapes
- let overlap, contact, crossing, containment, or continuation carry meaning
- preserve generous quiet space
- use one dominant silhouette that remains clear as a thumbnail
- prefer flat matte construction over realistic perspective, shading, or light

Do not copy another brand's palette, carrier shape, hand drawing, or published
composition. A Scholium image should remain identifiable when the background
hue changes because its ink character, hand gesture, paper interior, and
scholarly object language are stable.

## 3. Intent to geometry

Write the intent sentence first, then identify its structure:

| Intent structure | Physical relation | Useful geometry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| guidance without possession | offer or release | open palm, object between actors |
| inquiry that leaves a trace | draw or unwind | continuous line crossing a boundary |
| no determinate endpoint | continue beyond crop | path/thread exits frame without glow |
| choice under uncertainty | part or branch | one origin, unequal open branches |
| return and revision | retrace or turn | doubled line with visible earlier marks |
| comparison | hold or balance | two unlike forms sharing one axis |
| accumulation | gather or stack | repeated marks becoming one readable mass |
| revelation | lift, unfold, or part | obscured form made partially visible |

The accent belongs on the element that proves the intent. If removing it does
not weaken the proposition, it is decoration rather than a semantic accent.

## 4. Composition families

Choose one family per image:

### Gesture and object

One cuffed hand performs a decisive action on one scholarly or mythic object.
The object and contact point are large enough to read immediately. Use for
offering, releasing, opening, tracing, and weighing.

### Relational handoff

Two hands or a hand and a simplified figure exchange, guide, or orient around
one object. Keep the relation legible; do not create portrait drama.

### Journey trace

A simplified figure or hand moves through a boundary while a line, thread, or
series of manuscript marks records where inquiry has passed. Let the trace
continue beyond the crop when the process is unresolved.

### Boundary and opening

A hand parts, lifts, or crosses one large simple plane. The boundary is a
meaningful condition, not a decorative frame.

### Paired comparison

One hand or axis holds two contrasting forms. Differences should be structural,
not merely two colors or labels.

For a variant set, rotate families. Three versions of the same hand on three
background colors count as one idea, not three directions.

## 5. Shape system

- Use a full-frame opaque pigment field.
- Use warm paper-colored fills as parts of the subject: sleeve, page, thread
  carrier, path segment, labyrinth wall, or aperture. Do not default to one
  generic central ivory blob.
- Use deep ink contours and a few solid ink shapes.
- Keep only the principal shape families required by the intended relation.
- Allow intentional cropping at an edge when it communicates continuation.
- Preserve generous quiet field appropriate to the live destination. Increase
  quiet space around interface-copy overlays; decrease it only when the action
  becomes unreadable.
- Use no border, card frame, mock device, or paper sheet floating above the
  background.

## 6. Line and surface

- Contours are moderately bold, rounded, slightly dry, and controlled.
- Line weight may vary between outer contour, internal articulation, and tiny
  manuscript marks, but never becomes scratchy crosshatching.
- Forms are flat and front-facing or use only shallow overlap. Avoid cinematic
  perspective.
- Surface texture is subtle paper tooth or dry ink dropout visible only at
  close inspection.
- No pencil construction lines, charcoal smudge, watercolor wash, etching,
  stippled engraving, glossy vector precision, gradient, glow, or cast shadow.

## 7. Product-art palette

These are **candidate illustration fields**, not new interface Variables or
approved status colors. They stay inside the raster artwork and require human
visual acceptance before production promotion. When a current product surface
has a closed canonical palette or fixed composition, that live Design
authority wins; these candidates cannot expand the shipped surface.

Stable anchors:

- resolve current semantic Paper and Accent roles from live product authority;
  do not copy values from an old proof or this reference
- contour: the current icon's deep ink character; sample from the live asset or
  describe it as deep blue-black rather than inventing a new UI token

Candidate full-frame pigment families:

- mineral blue: reflective, spacious, exploratory
- verdigris green: patient, connective, restorative
- golden ochre: illuminating, archival, active
- oxblood: demanding, dialectical, consequential
- plum ash: ambiguous, contemplative, nocturnal
- vermilion: urgent, decisive, sparingly used

Use one field family per image, paper, ink, and at most one semantic accent.
Do not reproduce the Anthropic orange/blue/green rotation or assign permanent
meanings to these candidate hues. The intended action must remain legible in a
grayscale inspection.

## 8. Mythic adaptation

Verify the primary mythic object and action before drawing. Preserve at least
two identifiers, then state the modern philosophical interpretation separately.

- Verify which object, actor, and action are source-supported rather than
  selecting a myth by thematic resemblance alone.
- Keep the source-supported identifiers visible while treating any research,
  memory, method, or inquiry meaning as a modern interpretation unless the
  source states it.
- Do not introduce victory, punishment, homecoming, revelation, or a
  determinate endpoint unless the task and source support that consequence.

## 9. Prompt contract

Write four compact paragraphs:

1. **Delivery field:** destination, exact frame, full-frame opaque pigment,
   quiet-space share, crop-safe and copy-safe regions.
2. **Intent scene:** subject, decisive verb, object, contact or relation,
   semantic accent, visible consequence, and any off-frame continuation.
3. **Scholium rendering:** cuffed-hand/ink character where applicable, deep
   rounded contours, paper-colored flat shapes, simple geometry, subtle paper
   tooth, limited palette, no in-image text.
4. **Hard avoids:** exact failure modes from the brief plus common negatives.

Name concrete objects and verbs. Never rely on `Claude-like`, `beautiful`,
`philosophical`, `minimal`, or `hand-drawn` without rendering instructions.

## 10. Hard avoids

- exact app-icon pose, tracing, recolor, crop, or composition
- third-party logo, palette recipe, carrier shape, hand asset, mascot, or copied
  published composition
- realistic sketch, location reportage, fashion figure, portrait anatomy, or
  live-action still
- arbitrary paper scraps, floating dots, question mark, infinity sign, generic
  maze, lone road, or person merely standing
- unsupported endpoint, victory, punishment, or historical spectacle introduced
  as decoration
- children's-book cuteness, kawaii, anime, caricature, mascot, or comedy face
- glossy vector polish, 3D, gradient, hard shadow, cinematic light, neon,
  texture overload, collage clutter, or photoreal paper mockup
- logo, watermark, CTA, UI chrome, baked-in copy, or state conveyed by color
  alone

## 11. Visual inspection

At full size, inspect hand anatomy, contour continuity, accidental tangencies,
paper texture, and safe areas. Use the actual artwork slot when known; never
infer it from a window's initial size. At thumbnail size, ask:

1. What is the subject?
2. What verb is occurring?
3. Which shape proves the intent?

If any answer is unavailable, regenerate once with a larger gesture, fewer
secondary shapes, and more explicit contact. If the output reads as a sketch,
regenerate once with flat filled forms, bolder rounded contours, shallow
overlap, and explicit bans on pencil, charcoal, etching, and realism.
